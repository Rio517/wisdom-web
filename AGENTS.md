# Project instructions

## Purpose and scope

Wisdom is a working title for a selective, guided life manual for ages 8+ and for general sharing. The core explanation must be approachable from age eight, with depth for older readers. Treat readers as capable of substantial ideas. The first lesson explains the impact of choices and how their effects add up over time, alongside circumstances, support and chance. It does not teach the psychology of making decisions. Habits and daily practice is a separate lesson, next in priority; “How we make choices” follows it. Relationships and other life subjects are outside the first release.

Read `NEXT_STEP.md`, `docs/brief.md`, and the relevant research and proposal before working. Start research navigation at `docs/research/README.md`. The cross-topic synthesis and source index link to canonical topic findings and ledgers; do not duplicate them into one growing omnibus document.

## Content

- Preserve the central idea that learning and choices can compound and influence future options. Distinguish building on knowledge, related-skill transfer, and possible learning/enjoyment feedback; do not promise a growth rate or universal transfer.
- Distinguish evidence, editorial inference, fictional illustration, and open questions. Cite research claims near the claim and retain source details in the research ledger.
- Explain mechanisms and trade-offs. Avoid guaranteed success, invented probabilities, fixed life trajectories, and unsupported age cutoffs.
- Skills, qualifications, support, circumstances, and chance all matter. Academic performance is not a measure of human worth. Describe occupations respectfully.
- Use clear language with substantive reasoning; introduce useful vocabulary in context. Include recovery, asking for help, and changing strategy.
- Keep family names, private anecdotes, and the originating conversation out of public material.

## Documentation

- Keep research and plans in this repository under `docs/`. `docs/README.md` is the map.
- Keep the canonical lesson roadmap in `docs/README.md`, in this order: 01 choices and their effects, 02 habits/daily practice, 03 how we make choices, 04 happiness, 05 relationships, and 06 how to learn. Habits must have its own lesson, even if short. Friendship and romance are a possible split of relationships, not yet separate approved lessons. Each lesson with substantive work has a folder, `docs/<subject>-<lesson>/`, with a README that lists what belongs to it, `ideas/` for thinking that is not agreed, and `research/` for its questions and prompts. Findings go into the shared library under `docs/research/`; product documents stay in `docs/product/`. Ideas state their section, status and dates. Do not create empty future-lesson folders.
- Specific proposals belong in `docs/product/NNN-short-name.md`, with status, created date, and updated date. Maintain the product index. Keep IDs and filenames stable; use Git for revision history.
- Register design work in `docs/design/README.md` before creating folders. Group the Choices work under the stable study `docs/design/001-choices-explainer/`, with numbered review rounds (`vNN-purpose/`) and explicit artifact revisions (`name-vNN.png`). Refine within the existing round; a styling variation is not a new top-level study. Create another round only for a distinct review question and record its purpose, date and status in the study index. Never overwrite a reviewed image or use unversioned `final`/`latest` filenames for new artifacts. Preserve historical captures inside their frozen numbered rounds.
- Every design round needs a README identifying its question, related product documents, current review images and exact prompt/source files. Give direct clickable image links when requesting visual review; a documentation link or invisible embedded preview is not sufficient.
- Write reference documents as current statements. Clearly label proposals and unapproved decisions.
- `NEXT_STEP.md` is the current handoff: state, immediate work, unresolved decisions, and verified tooling. Replace stale status instead of appending a diary.
- `COMPLETED.md` is the concise chronological log. Update it only for work actually done.
- Keep one canonical source for each substantive topic and link to it from summaries.
- Research is grouped into learning, lifetime opportunities, circumstances/chance, decision-making/development, and habits, with delivery references separate. Each bucket needs findings, linked sources, evidence limits, and open questions. Habits currently has an initial formation evidence base, not a completed child habit-change review.

## Delivery and quality

- All project development, preview and test servers must use ports 4600–4699, bound to 127.0.0.1. The site uses 4600 for development and 4601 for build preview; the earlier prototype studies use 4602 and 4603 (`npm run dev:prototype`). The shared guard is `tooling/ports.js`. Fail clearly on a port conflict; never silently fall back outside the allowed range.
- Delegate bounded work to simpler, lower-cost models where appropriate. Use a capable mid-tier model for multi-file implementation and reviews; reserve higher-capability models for design judgment or genuinely difficult findings. Do not lower quality or duplicate reviews merely to parallelize.

- The production site is an Astro static site: pages in `src/pages/[...locale]/` (one set of pages built for every language), layouts and navigation in `src/layouts/` and `src/components/`, lesson code in `src/lessons/`, the shared path engine in `src/engine/`, site data in `src/data/site.js` and design tokens in `src/styles/tokens.css`. It is intended for `https://wisdom.knyflores.com` through GitHub Pages (`public/CNAME`); publishing and DNS need the owner's go-ahead. On 2026-09-27 the owner approved the guided journey (product 013) as Lesson 1. The code under `prototype/` holds earlier studies, not the website.
- The site is multilingual (product 014): English is the source and is served at `/`; Spanish, German and French are under `/es/`, `/de/` and `/fr/`. No user-facing prose belongs in code. Words live in `src/i18n/messages/<locale>.json` (flat semantic keys, ICU MessageFormat, whole sentences, no HTML), and long-form pages live in `src/content/pages/<locale>/`. A change to English text updates every catalog in the same change; `npm test` enforces key and argument parity. A language stays `draft` in `src/i18n/config.js` (built, unlinked, `noindex`) until a fluent reader has reviewed it; only the owner publishes one. Check German layouts after copy changes.
- Keep a static multipage site with real page URLs (`docs/product/002-delivery-architecture.md`). Only written lessons get pages or navigation links; planned lessons may appear in the roadmap and index as “in preparation” without links.
- Guided progression, explanatory animation, optional examples/deep dives, and a selectable starting age on the life map are product requirements. The selected age describes an example, not a diagnosis or prediction of the reader's life. Video is a possible later medium.
- The first entry should be a short, reader-controlled click-through explainer: roughly 2–3 minutes for one clear takeaway, with optional examples and deep dives available without overwhelming the main path. A possible second entry may let a reader choose an example age and receive relevant fictional examples; looking back to age eight is allowed only as an explicitly labeled part of an older fictional example. Never imply that the reader personally did those things. Age-range routing and a fully redesigned short lesson are next-stage scope unless the existing controls can express them.
- Visuals must explain an idea accurately. Interactive diagrams need keyboard operation, a readable static alternative, and reduced-motion support.
- The opening visual must first communicate an abundance of irregular branching possibilities from the beginning of life. Ages 8+ describes the reading audience, not the start of the life map. Avoid numbers inside life-path dots and numbered endpoint badges. The approved v06 composition is the static visual direction for the current flat code pass; technical verification of the prototype is not production visual approval.
- Establish a faithful baseline of the supplied reference before modernizing it. Preserve its structure across genuinely distinct design treatments, not merely changes of texture or tint. Once a direction is chosen, refine one visual property at a time. The desired eventual finish is clean and modern; rough penwork, paper texture and botanical variations are not an approved direction. Reference fidelity studies can retain the original labels, colors and Today divider without making those final website decisions.
- The sculptural treatment in design study 001, round v05 is selected for refinement, not approved as a production asset. The current artwork request preserves the dark-green lived route, makes gray strokes even and slightly thicker, places a smaller key at the top right and Today at the bottom, and extends branching gray alternatives beyond Today above and below green futures with a soft edge fade. The divider is not a barrier. This image is illustrative, not a measured count of opportunities. Preserve the reference baseline and earlier versions for comparison. The approved v06 composition may now be implemented as the flat code study; that implementation is not production approval.
- The approved v06-style composition is a code reference, not a raster asset to ship as the UI. Keep the opening map completely flat: no dimensionality, shadows, highlights, bevels, gradients that imply volume, or shaded dots. Gray and green routes use uniform stroke color and width; branch-level alpha fades are allowed as a flat visibility treatment for the retained/fading/hybrid comparison, in addition to a page-edge opacity mask.
- Generate the path field as a connected, seeded network. Every branch has independent bend and split decisions; shared crowding awareness and bounded resource use are the only cross-branch constraints. Do not reintroduce synchronized waves, hardcoded age coordinates or repeated overdraw of shared trunks. Derive Today from age along the selected route. Completed history is dark green, reachable futures light green, and untaken branches the faintest gray; descendants of an untaken branch remain untaken even beyond Today. These states describe the fictional scenario, not permanently closed real-life possibilities.
- A standalone tuning page exists at `prototype/path-lab.html`. The owner reports a useful visual state; preserve it while the explanation is developed. Earlier statements that no tuning UI exists are stale. This is not production approval. Fading remains a flat presentation treatment, not measured real-life reachability or volume. Read `NEXT_STEP.md` for the active task and product 010 for the earlier engine proposal and boundaries.
- Keep network generation separate from scenario assumptions and choice-point annotations. Stable choice-point IDs allow explicit selections and later authored examples without changing the network. Stability assumes the same seed, configuration and algorithm version. Drawing probabilities and resource caps are not estimates of human opportunity. The reusable engine is in scope; a scenario editor or simulator UI is not.
- Use project-specific design tokens and readable type. The minimum viewport targets are desktop and iPad mini; phone design is deferred. Verify rendered pages at the required desktop and tablet sizes before claiming visual quality.
- Use Tailwind by default for layout and UI styling in new pages and implementation work. The owner permits the Tailwind CDN for local development and quick studies when a compilation step is unnecessary. Use compiled Tailwind for production. Custom CSS remains appropriate for small exceptions; Canvas/SVG still own diagram drawing. Preserve reviewed artifacts rather than retroactively restyling them.
- Use the clean field-guide direction and collapsible navigation in product document 003. Keep the implemented abundant path field and the beginning-to-today travel followed by a modest zoom. Routes move forward without loops or backward curls; untaken gray routes and the today divider stay visible. Preserve a dark-green traveled route and lighter gray-green futures. Tailwind supplies layout, Canvas 2D supplies routes, and semantic HTML supplies labels and controls; Three.js is not needed. This diagram is not a literal process stepper. Motion must be quick, interruptible and readable when settled, without delaying content.
- Lesson 1 is a guided journey (product 013): the life map first, then Alfredo’s hike, Maya’s two paths, the ten-afternoon game, the life-path explorer and takeaways. Every chapter is reachable from the chapter trail and the text version. The live English words are `src/i18n/messages/en.json`; `docs/content/choices-journey.md` is the readable lesson copy generated from it (`npm run copy:lesson`); product documents 007–012 and `docs/content/choices-story.md` record earlier studies.
- Layer the story to show repeated choices, accumulated understanding, and circumstances or decisions outside the person's control. Distinguish learning, practical setup and behavior-specific habits; do not claim a universal habit of good judgment. A simulator is possible future scope, not infrastructure to build now. The core explanation must work before expanding it.
- Keep decision psychology out of the Choices–Paths lesson. The rider-and-elephant model and moral matrix in Jonathan Haidt's *The Righteous Mind* are owner-preferred leads for the later “How we make choices” research, not established findings or a request to research them now. Record future leads in the lesson roadmap until there is enough work to justify another lesson folder.
- The later decision-making research should also cover principled action versus convenience, creative option generation, design thinking and alternatives, and when constraints help or prematurely exclude possibilities. Preserve these as goals and research questions, not claims that most people are irrational or that constraints always harm creativity.
- Keep relationships, conflict, and de-escalation as a dedicated future subject rather than adding them to the choices/learning lesson. A future end quiz is only an idea, not current scope. “What makes a happy life?” is a future deep-research question; do not research it or invent evidence in the current phase.
- Preserve user changes. Keep Git add, commit, and push as separate operations. Avoid unnecessary workflow or infrastructure copied from other projects.
- Run checks proportionate to the change. Research documents need citation, link, consistency, and scope checks; interactive features need behavioral verification.
