## Idea

Two new parts of the site, beside the lessons:

- **Thinking tools:** small tools a child, and the parent reading along, can carry into a real day. Each one fits on one screen.
- **Deciding** (working name): a separate, advanced section (ages 11 and up) about why people reach different answers, including the moral matrix from Jonathan Haidt's *The Righteous Mind* (2012). It comes after Lesson 3, The elephant and the rider, and builds on it, but it is not part of that lesson.

## Thinking tools

### Card

One screen per tool:

- one picture, as minimal as the lessons' pictures;
- one example from a child's day;
- one small activity, done in about thirty seconds;
- one line starting "Doesn't work when…";
- one line for the grown-up;
- an evidence label: **Well tested**, **Rule of thumb** or **Debated**.

### First tools

| Order | Tool | Example | Label | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | If-then plans | "If I get home, then I read first." | Well tested in adults, promising in school | Gollwitzer and Sheeran 2006 (meta-analysis, mostly adults); Wang, Wang and Gai 2021 (mental contrasting, g ≈ 0.24–0.34, three child studies) |
| 2 | One-way and two-way doors | A new club can be undone; a mean message can't. | Rule of thumb | Bezos, Amazon shareholder letter 2015 ("one-way doors", Type 1 and Type 2 decisions) |
| 3 | And then what? | All the candy now, and then what? Paired with what you give up (opportunity cost). | Rule of thumb; opportunity-cost neglect is well tested in adults | Hardin; Frederick et al. 2009 |
| 4 | Circle of control | Rain on sports day; a raincoat in the bag. | Rule of thumb | Epictetus, *Enchiridion* 1. A recap of Lesson 1's sort. |
| 5 | Name the feeling | "I'm nervous." | Debated | Lieberman et al. 2007 (30 adults). No brain claims. |
| 6 | Benefit of the doubt | A friend didn't wave; they didn't see you. | Rule of thumb | Hostile attribution research (Dodge) |
| 7 | Sunk cost | Staying in a boring game because you've played an hour. "Would I start this today?" | Well tested in adults | Arkes and Blumer 1985. Young children show it less than adults (Arkes and Ayton 1999), so it is told as a trap grown-ups fall into. |

### Boundaries

- A tool is a tool, not a law. Every card says when it does not work.
- Circle of control never blames a child for what is not theirs to change; its card ends with asking for help.
- Sunk cost sits beside Lesson 2's practice: it says when to keep going (you still want it) and when to stop (you'd not start it today).
- No marshmallow-test story, no "growth mindset" promises, no brain claims.

## Deciding

### Lessons

1. **Gut first.** We often feel the answer first and find the reasons after. Builds on the elephant and the rider (Haidt 2001, "The emotional dog and its rational tail").
2. **Different good things.** People care about kindness, fairness, loyalty, respect, what is sacred and freedom, and the people around us teach us which ones come first. Haidt calls a group's shared set a moral matrix: it binds people together and can blind them to other matrices (*The Righteous Mind*, Part III, "Morality Binds and Blinds").
3. **Hearing the other side.** Saying someone's view so well they would agree with it, and giving the benefit of the doubt.

### Boundaries

- No political words and no group labels. Two good things in conflict instead: a friend cheated, and loyalty pulls against fairness.
- No questionnaire and no score. The moral foundations questionnaire does not hold up well across countries (Iurino and Saucier 2020, 27 countries), and the foundations list itself has changed (fairness split into equality and proportionality in 2023; liberty is a proposal).
- "In many surveys", never "liberals are… / conservatives are…".
- Intuition first and reasons after is shown as a picture of what often happens, not as how the brain works.
