## Lesson 2 · Practice

- **Idea:** nobody chooses to be a soccer player or a mathematician in one day. They choose to go to practice, to go again, to focus while they are there, to practise at home; to take the science class and do the homework, again and again. Those small choices are how the big things happen.
- **Boundary:** effort makes a good result much more likely, not certain. Lesson 1's line "Practice helps, but it doesn't guarantee a result" stays, said once ([learning findings](../research/learning/README.md)).
- **Hero picture:** one of Lesson 1's long green paths, then a zoom in. The path is made of footprints, and each footprint is one small choice.
- **Beats:** Maya wants to be good at soccer, and going to practice is the choice she can make today · the small choices · a long time when nothing seems to happen · skills build on skills · the same holds for anything, such as maths · basketball at eleven, where some skills carry over · one line on practised things taking less effort (a seed for Lesson 3) · never too late, and real life is messier.
- **Activity:** ten afternoons, three picture choices a day, skill boxes that grow; a second activity opens later with its boxes partly filled, so related skills are seen to carry over. Picked on 2026-10-17 from three drawn ideas.

## Lesson 3 · The elephant and the rider

- **Idea:** a small rider on a big elephant. The rider plans and looks ahead; the elephant is strong and fast, carries feelings and does what it has learned. The two are one team. The picture comes from Jonathan Haidt (*The Happiness Hypothesis*, 2006; *The Righteous Mind*, 2012).
- **Boundary:** an illustration, not a description of the brain. Not "willpower is a muscle that tires" (ego depletion failed large replications), not the rider as the real self, not feelings as the enemy, and no claim that the picture itself helps children regulate themselves (untested).
- **Beats:** meet the pair · what practice trained (Maya's feet) · what the rider does well (Alfredo's water plan, Lesson 1's what-ifs) · when they want different things, pulling the reins works for a moment · people with good self-control tend to rely on helpful habits more than on resisting (adult, correlational) · for older readers, the elephant sometimes decides first and the rider finds a reason afterwards.
- **Activity (candidate):** steering the elephant past a tempting side trail, by pulling the reins or by changing the path.

## Lesson 4 · Habits

- **Idea:** a habit is a worn path. It helps or gets in the way, judged by the reader's own goals; the lesson does not call habits good or bad.
- **Picture:** Lesson 1's map style, with trails wearing in where feet keep stepping and grassing over where they stop.
- **Beats:** starting gets easier while doing it well still takes thought · a path made without meaning to · shaping the path, with if-then plans · a missed day is not starting over · changing a path · healthy habits such as sleep, moving and time outside, without weight or food judgments.
- **Activity:** 015's two weeks; by the second week the elephant heads for the usual activity on its own, and the reader steers only to change it. Then the reader's own plan, on paper.
- **Research still needed:** children's health habits, before any copy on sleep, movement or food.
