## Many paths

### Paths and choices

Your choices shape where a life can go. Good and bad luck, which nobody chooses, shape it too.

First we watch one whole life in half a minute. Then we go on a hike with Alfredo and his dad.

Picture: A single dot marks the beginning of a life.

### One of many paths

The story of Sam, one line a step (the lives store, src/lessons/choices/lives/; another life plays on a return visit or after “Watch another life”):

- **Age 0** Sam is born.
- **Age 6** His parents sign him up for choir.
- **Age 10** He picks the guitar.
- **Age 14** He starts a band with two friends.
- **Age 16** The three bandmates record a first song.
- **Age 16** Both friends quit, and he is on his own.
- **Age 17** He forms a new band with three others.
- **Age 18** Luckily, a radio DJ plays one of his songs.
- **Age 20** A record company offers the band a deal.
- **Age 22** He goes on a world tour.
- **Age 24** Drinking and drugs take over, and his shows fall apart.
- **Age 25** The record company ends the deal, and he loses his flat.
- **Age 26** He asks for help, and a friend lets him stay.
- **Age 30** He teaches guitar and writes new songs.
- **Age 33** He has two great kids.
- **Age 36** He teaches music at the local school.
- **Age 41** He watches his daughter sing on stage.
- Many paths are still ahead.

In the lesson, you can change one of Sam’s choices and watch his path change.

### Choices add up.

Choir led Sam to the guitar. The guitar led to a band, and the band recorded a song. Luck lifted his path, and a hard year pushed it down. Some things were not his choice. Now let’s look at choices over just a few days.

Picture: The map shows Sam’s whole path with three labels. “Picks guitar” is labelled His choice. “A DJ plays the song” is labelled Luck. “The band splits” is labelled Not his choice.

## The hike

### Alfredo makes a plan.

Alfredo and his dad are getting ready for a big hike. They study the map. Then they pack snacks, jackets and a bottle of water each.

Mirror Lake is a long way up. Nobody gets there in one jump. It’s one small step after another.

Picture: A trail map shows the trail climbing from the trailhead through a forest, past a stream and up a ridge to Mirror Lake. Alfredo and his dad wait at the trailhead with full water bottles.

### Half the water is gone.

Partway up, Alfredo checks his bottle. Half the water is gone already. The lake is still far away.

**What should they do?**

- **Keep going to the lake** — They’d reach the lake with almost no water for the long walk home. On a hot day, that’s dangerous. Dad says, “The lake isn’t going anywhere. Let’s turn back.”
- **Turn back now** (what they chose) — That’s good thinking. The other half of the water is for getting home safely.

Picture: The walkers climb to the big rock. Their water bottles are half full, and the lake is still far away.

### Turning back isn’t giving up.

They walk home without seeing the lake. Alfredo is disappointed. So is his dad.

But they learned something for next time. They’ll bring enough water to get there and back.

Backpack adds: Water for there and back

Picture: The walkers return to the trailhead. The backpack gains a new card, called Water for there and back.

### Practice builds on practice.

Over the next few weeks, they take short walks near home. Each walk starts from what Alfredo learned on the last one.

- **Reading the map.** He finds where they are on the map, then picks the way.
- **Spotting landmarks.** Landmarks are things you see from far away, like a big rock.
- **Resting before you’re tired.** They stop for a drink before their legs get heavy.

Backpack adds: Reading the map · Spotting landmarks · Resting before you’re tired

Picture: Alfredo and his dad stand at the trailhead. The backpack gains three cards, called Reading the map, Spotting landmarks and Resting before you’re tired.

### The second try.

This time they carry more water and plan where to rest. Alfredo spots the big rock and the stream from the map.

They aren’t starting from zero. Everything they learned comes with them.

Picture: The walkers climb past the place where they turned back last time. They have plenty of water and reach the bridge over the stream.

### Something they couldn’t control.

Last night, a storm washed part of the lake trail away. A ranger at the bridge says it’s closed. No plan can stop the rain.

**What now?**

- **Sneak past the sign** — The sign is there because the trail isn’t safe. Sneaking past could turn a bad day into a dangerous one.
- **Go home** — Feeling disappointed is normal. Alfredo feels it too, and going home is allowed. But is there another good choice?
- **Ask the ranger about other trails** (what they chose) — The ranger smiles. “Have you seen the Waterfall Trail? It’s shorter, and it’s open.” It’s a lucky tip they never planned for.

Picture: Rain clouds hang over the ridge. The lake trail beyond the bridge is marked closed. A ranger stands at the bridge.

### Plans change. You can respond.

They take the Waterfall Trail and eat lunch by the falls. Alfredo says it’s the best sandwich of his life. The lake can wait.

That’s called resilience. You can’t control everything, but you can find a good way forward.

> **Does resilience mean never stopping?**
>
> No, it doesn’t. Turning back, resting, asking for help or picking a new goal can all be good choices.
>
> Alfredo can feel disappointed and still decide what to do next. Nobody has to pretend a setback feels good.

Picture: The walkers follow the open Waterfall Trail to a lookout beside a waterfall.

## Explore

### Choose a life path.

Start at the beginning and pick a choice at each fork. Some doors close, and some open by surprise. You can always go back.

#### Example choices by age

- **3–6 (chosen by family):** Swimming lessons · Bedtime stories every night · Nursery with friends · Two languages at home · Singing and music at home · Playing outside every day · Lots of time with grandparents · A dance class · Puzzles and building blocks · Library visits every week · Helping to bake
- **7–10:** Join the chess club · Try gymnastics · Art club after school · Learn to code with a friend · Join the Scouts · Get a tutor for maths · Practise times tables · Start violin · Grow vegetables · Learn to skateboard · Join the choir
- **11–14:** Apply for the gifted programme · Get a maths tutor · Start a second language · Volunteer at the animal shelter · Join drama club · Switch from piano to drums · Draw every day · Take up running · Quit a club that isn’t fun · Help look after a younger brother · Start a school newspaper
- **15–18:** Take the science subjects · Get a part-time job · Focus on art · Study hard for exams · Take a trade course · Join the debate team · Captain the team · Learn to drive · Choose history and languages · Make music with friends · Take a break from sport
- **19–25:** Go to university · Start an apprenticeship · Travel for a year · Start working · Move to a new city · Study music · Train to teach · Work and study part-time · Stay near family
- **26–40:** Change careers · Start a family · Go back to study · Move abroad · Take on a big project at work · Build something of your own · Take a job closer to family · Train for a marathon · Learn a new language · Care for a parent · Lead a team · Work fewer hours · Buy a small house
- **41–55:** Learn something new · Mentor someone younger · Start a new hobby · Switch to part-time · Move to the countryside · Volunteer locally · Take a year off · Go back to school · Start a community garden · Learn to paint · Retrain for a new job
- **56–70:** Retire · Keep working a while longer · Volunteer · Travel · Teach others your craft · Spend time with grandchildren · Start painting · Join a choir · Grow a garden · Write your story

Closed-way reasons (children): No places left this year · It cost too much right now · It was too far away · It stopped running this year · The family moved · Nobody could take you there

Closed-way reasons (adults): Money was tight that year · It wasn’t offered nearby · Someone in the family needed care · An injury got in the way · The timing didn’t work out · Someone else got the place

## Takeaways

### What to take with you.

Your choices matter. They’re just not the whole story. Lots of paths are still open.

Alfredo got what he wanted by choosing to practise. Next: how practice works.

- **Choices add up.** Small things you do again and again can grow into something big, for better or worse. One hard day doesn’t decide your future.
- **Not everything is yours to choose.** Some surprises are storms. Some are lucky breaks. Either way, you can pause, ask for help and choose your next step.
