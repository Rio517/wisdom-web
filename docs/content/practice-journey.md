## Small choices

### Wanting and choosing.

Maya is eight, and she wants to be good at soccer. But she can’t choose to be good today.

She can choose something smaller. Practice is at four o’clock, and she can go.

Picture: Maya stands with her ball at the start of a long path. Far along it, a small flag says Good at soccer. At her feet, an outlined footprint says Practice at four.

### Again and again.

So she goes. The next week she goes again, even in the rain.

At practice, she listens to the coach and tries hard. At home, she kicks her ball against the wall.

Each of these is one small choice.

Picture: Maya four times along one line of footprints: at practice, at practice in the rain, with her eyes on the ball, and kicking her ball against the wall at home.

### A long, quiet time.

For a long time, it feels like nothing is happening. The ball still slips past her foot.

Something is happening, though. Each practice leaves a little behind, even before she can see it.

Picture: Maya’s weeks of practice: season 1 is almost full, and seasons 2 and 3 are still empty. Beside them is her soccer box. Ball control is learning; passing and knowing where to stand are just starting.

## Adding up

### One season, then three.

Season after season, Maya keeps choosing practice. From far away, three years of it look like one long line.

Let’s look closer.

Picture: Maya’s life on the map, from birth to today. A dark green line passes Starts school, Joins a soccer team and Tries basketball, with gray paths she didn’t take and light green paths ahead. A box marks her soccer years.

### Step by step.

Up close, the line is made of footprints. Each footprint is one small choice.

Most of them are the same choice, made again. No single step made Maya better. Together, they did.

Picture: The box on the map opens into a large view of the same stretch. Up close, the green line is a trail of footprints, labelled Practice, Practice again, Practice in the rain, Wall at home and Eyes on the ball.

### Skills on skills.

Something else adds up too. Once Maya stops staring at the ball, she can look up.

Looking up helps her learn passing. Passing teaches her where to stand. Each skill gives the next one a place to start.

Picture: Three blocks like steps, each resting on the one before: Looking up, then Passing, then Knowing where to stand. Maya’s footprints climb them, and she stands on the top step.

### Maths too.

The same goes for many things. Nobody becomes a mathematician just by deciding to.

A mathematician chooses maths class. They do the homework, then do it again the next night. Years of small choices like that are what a mathematician is made of.

Picture: Someone else’s trail of footprints, labelled Maths class, Homework, Homework again and A hard problem. It ends at a chalkboard: Mathematician.

## Something new

### A new ball.

At eleven, Maya tries basketball. Some of what she built comes with her.

She already knows where to stand and when to pass. Dribbling with her hands is new, so she starts that from the beginning.

Picture: Maya at eleven with a basketball, under a hoop. Her basketball box: knowing where to stand and passing start partly filled with soccer’s stripes, and dribbling with hands is just starting.

### Never too late.

Anyone can start practising, at any age. Starting later can mean more to catch up on, but the door stays open.

Real life is messier than this picture. Coaches, time and money matter. So does luck, good and bad. Practice helps, but it doesn’t guarantee a result.

Picture: The map again. One path ahead turns green later in life and grows its own footprints: A later start. On the other paths ahead, a star marks A coach notices, and a diamond marks An injury.

## Activity

### Ten afternoons.

Story column, before afternoon 7: Pick one thing each afternoon. Your skills grow from what you pick.

From afternoon 7: A new box opens partly filled. Some of your soccer skills come with you.

After the tenth: Every one of your ten afternoons left something behind.

- **Afternoon 1 of 10 · Monday.** Soccer practice is at four. The cello waits in the corner. Go to soccer practice (Soccer) · Practise cello (Cello) · Watch videos (Rest and fun)
- **Afternoon 2 of 10 · Tuesday.** The cello lesson is today. School was long. Go to your lesson (Cello) · Kick at the wall (Soccer) · Take a nap (Rest and fun)
- **Afternoon 3 of 10 · Wednesday.** Your friends are playing tag in the park. Play tag (Rest and fun) · Practise cello (Cello) · Kick at the wall (Soccer)
- **Afternoon 4 of 10 · Thursday.** It’s raining, so soccer practice is off. Dribble in the hallway (Soccer) · Practise cello (Cello) · Build a blanket fort (Rest and fun)
- **Afternoon 5 of 10 · Friday.** Soccer practice is today. A new film just came out. Go to soccer practice (Soccer) · Watch the new film (Rest and fun) · Practise cello (Cello)
- **Afternoon 6 of 10 · Monday.** You slept badly, and you’re tired. Rest (Rest and fun) · Go to soccer practice (Soccer) · Practise cello (Cello)
- **Afternoon 7 of 10 · Wednesday · New.** A friend asks you to shoot hoops at the park. Play basketball (Basketball) · Kick at the wall (Soccer) · Practise cello (Cello)
- **Afternoon 8 of 10 · Thursday.** There’s soccer practice, but a friend asks you over. Go to soccer practice (Soccer) · Go to your friend’s (Rest and fun) · Practise cello (Cello)
- **Afternoon 9 of 10 · Friday.** The coach is teaching a new passing trick. Learn the new trick (Soccer) · Practise cello (Cello) · Rest (Rest and fun)
- **Afternoon 10 of 10 · Saturday.** It’s the last afternoon. The team needs one more player. Play in the match (Soccer) · Practise cello (Cello) · Read a comic (Rest and fun)

End card: **Ten afternoons later**, a line each for soccer and cello by how often they were picked (for example “You chose soccer 5 times, so soccer grew the most.”), then “Basketball began with a head start from soccer.” and **Start again**.

Picture: One afternoon’s card with three picture choices, and a column of your ten afternoons beside it. Your soccer and cello boxes grow from what you pick.

## Takeaways

### Three ideas to keep.

- **Big things start small.** Going to practice, again and again, is how big things happen.
- **Skills build on skills.** What you know gives the next thing a place to start.
- **Start any time.** Your next small choice is one you can make today.

Picture: The map once more, with Maya’s soccer years drawn as the footprints they are.

### Coming next.

What Maya practises gets easier over time.

These days, she doesn’t think about her feet at all. So who is doing it?
