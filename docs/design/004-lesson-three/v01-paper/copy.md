## Story

### Chapter · The pair

#### 01 pair · Rider and elephant.

Picture a small rider on a big elephant. The rider plans ahead. The elephant is strong and quick. It carries feelings and does what it has learned.

The picture comes from Jonathan Haidt. Many people find it useful. The real brain works in other ways.

Picture: the hero. A big, calm elephant stands on a wide path that runs on toward low hills. A small rider sits on its neck, one hand resting on its head, looking ahead. No reins.

- rider: Rider
- elephant: Elephant

### Chapter · Two strengths

#### 02 quick · Strong and quick.

Maya doesn’t think about her feet anymore. Practice trained her elephant, and now it does the dribbling.

The elephant pulls your hand back from a hot pan before you think. It can feel that something is off before you know why.

Picture: Lesson 2’s last picture, picked up again. Maya dribbles with her eyes up, and her elephant walks right behind her. Two small moments wait further along the same ground: a pan on a stove, and a wobbly plank over a stream.

- feet: Trained feet
- pan: Hot pan
- off: Something feels off

#### 03 slow · Slow and careful.

The rider is good at things that take time. It thinks ahead, makes plans and imagines what might happen.

Alfredo’s rider pictured what would happen if they kept going, so they turned back. Next time it planned water for there and back.

Picture: the trail map from Lesson 1’s hike, with Alfredo and his dad at the trailhead. A dotted what-if runs on to the lake and ends at an empty bottle. Their backpack card is beside them.

- whatif: Keep going
- back: Turn back
- card: Water for there and back

### Chapter · Steering

#### 04 reins · Pulling the reins.

Sometimes the two want different things. Your elephant wants one more game. Your rider thinks of homework.

Pulling the reins turns the elephant for a moment. But it is much bigger, and soon it drifts back. That’s no way to travel every day.

Picture: a fork in the path. The side trail leads to a screen with a game on it; the main path leads to a table with books. The elephant stands at the fork, nearer the side trail, and the rider holds a rein. A dotted line shows the elephant turning toward the books, then curving back.

- game: One more game
- home: Homework

#### 05 path · Easier paths.

People who seem good at this tend not to fight the elephant much. They train it with practice, and they choose easy paths.

So you put the game away and your books on the table. Now the easy path leads to homework.

Picture: the same fork. The screen is gone from the side trail, which fades into grass. The books are out on the table. The elephant walks the main path, and the rider’s hand rests on its head again.

- away: Game put away
- ready: Books ready

### Chapter · One team

#### 06 team · One team.

Neither one is the real you. The rider isn’t the boss, and the elephant isn’t the problem.

Jonathan Haidt says it like this. “We are the rider, and we are the elephant.”

They travel best together.

Picture: the elephant and its rider walking together up a gentle rise toward a lookout, the path behind them.

#### 07 first · Reasons afterwards.

Here is a stranger idea. Sometimes the elephant decides first. Then the rider finds a reason afterwards, and believes it.

A new food looks odd, and you push it away. Asked why, your rider says you aren’t hungry.

Picture: the elephant has already turned its back on a plate. Up on its neck, the rider gives a reason.

- kicker: For older readers · optional
- reason: Not hungry

### Chapter · Takeaways

#### 08 keep · Three ideas to keep.

- **Two parts, one team.** You are the rider and the elephant, and they work best together.
- **The elephant learns.** It learns from what you do again and again.
- **Train and choose the path.** The rider’s best tools are training the elephant and choosing its path.

Picture: the elephant and its rider on the open path again, small in a wide landscape.

#### 09 next · Coming next.

The elephant loves paths it already knows. Those paths are habits, and they come next.

Picture: the elephant walks along a well-worn trail that it has walked many times.

- button: Next: Lesson 4
