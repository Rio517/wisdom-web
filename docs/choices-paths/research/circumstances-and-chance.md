# Research: circumstances, setbacks and chance

Section: Choices › The paths we make
Status: focused review complete; lesson copy awaits review. Created: 2026-09-14. Updated: 2026-09-14.

## Why

The lesson needs to explain both what a person does and what happens outside their control: family circumstances, other people's decisions, setbacks, support and chance. The [lifetime findings](../../research/lifetime/README.md) already cover some barriers and later recovery. This research brings together the wider evidence about outside conditions and their effects on opportunities.

The [hiking proposal](../ideas/paths-worn-by-steps.md) needs that evidence before its ideas become lesson copy. The map's branching, narrowing and ending paths are illustrations, not measurements of how many opportunities a person has. Research should test the claims behind the story, not justify the current drawing or prove that a metaphor works.

Choices–Paths explains consequences and accumulation. The prompt also asks about scarcity, stress and children's understanding so authors can avoid misleading claims. Those background findings do not expand this lesson into the psychology of making decisions; that is a separate future lesson.

## Prompt

Copy the block below into a fresh research session in this repository.

```text
You are researching for Wisdom, a guided life manual for readers aged 8 and up.
Read AGENTS.md, docs/brief.md, docs/research/README.md and
docs/research/sources.md first, and follow the research conventions there:
one topic page with findings, product use and open questions; a source ledger
beside it with, for every source, the publication date, access date, study
population, design, measured outcome, confidence, and limitations; a note when
only an abstract was available; links and short paraphrases, never copied
papers or figures.

Topic: how circumstances, setbacks and chance shape the paths of a life,
alongside what a person does.

Answer these questions, in this order:

1. Which established frameworks describe how a life's options are shaped by
   things the person did not choose? Check at least: life-course theory
   (linked lives, timing of events, agency within constraints, turning
   points); cumulative advantage and disadvantage; chance events in career
   development ("planned happenstance" and later work); the capability
   approach (what a person is actually able to do given their
   circumstances); and Bronfenbrenner's ecological systems. For each: what
   it claims, what evidence supports it, what it cannot say.
2. What is the evidence that a setback or outside event changes later
   options, and that people recover or turn? Look for turning-point studies
   and resilience research on protective factors in childhood. Note what the
   lifetime bucket already covers (displaced workers, adult study) and link
   to it rather than repeating it.
3. How do constraints narrow the choices a person can see or make? Check
   scarcity and "bandwidth" research and its replications, and stress and
   decision-making. Report disagreements and failed replications plainly.
4. How is luck versus effort understood by children at different ages, and
   how have others taught circumstance to children without blame or
   fatalism? Look for developmental research on children's attributions and
   for any evaluated child-facing material.
5. Which of these claims are solid enough for an explanation a child can
   read, and how should "what moves you that you did not choose" be phrased
   for that reader? Give a short list of statements the lesson may make,
   each tied to a source ID, and a short list it must not make.

Deliverables:
- docs/research/circumstances/README.md: findings, product use, evidence
  limits, open questions, in the style of docs/research/lifetime/README.md.
- docs/research/circumstances/sources.md: the ledger, with new source IDs
  prefixed C (C1, C2, ...).
- Update the bucket table in docs/research/README.md and the ID table and
  the "Remaining evidence gaps" row in docs/research/sources.md.
- A short note at the end of docs/choices-paths/research/circumstances-and-chance.md
  saying the research is done and what changed.

Rules:
- Distinguish evidence, editorial inference and open questions.
- No invented probabilities, no fixed life trajectories, no ranking of lives
  or occupations, no claim that a person's circumstances decide their worth.
- Prefer primary sources and systematic reviews; note when a popular book is
  the only accessible source.
- Stop when each question has either a supported answer, a documented
  disagreement, or a recorded gap. Do not pad.
- Do not change lesson copy, product documents or code. That comes after
  review.
```

## After the research

Review the claims in the [hiking proposal](../ideas/paths-worn-by-steps.md): preparation can help later, repeated experience can support learning, outside conditions matter, and support or a changed route can help without guaranteeing recovery. Keep the account of choices' effects separate from an explanation of how people make decisions.

Evidence for these claims does not show that children understand a particular analogy. Test that separately with readers. Then decide the lesson copy and the unresolved meaning of height on the map. Hiking toward a summit must not turn vertical position into a ranking of people's lives.

## Completed review — 2026-09-14

The [findings](../../research/circumstances/README.md) answer all five questions, with [22 source records, C1–C22](../../research/circumstances/sources.md). The library and source indexes now link to this bucket. Existing lifetime studies are cross-linked rather than duplicated.

The review distinguishes frameworks from causal evidence, contrasting findings after parental job loss, possible support for recovery, and disagreements across scarcity studies. Short experiments with children inform possible explanations but do not establish lasting effects of this manual. Some sources were accessible only as abstracts; the ledger says which. This is a focused review, not exhaustive coverage.

The proposed hiking story remains unapproved. No lesson copy or visualization code changed as part of the research. Habits and the psychology of making decisions remain separate later lessons; the research here does not complete either topic.
