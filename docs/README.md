### Future research: How we make choices

Record these leads for later; they do not broaden the current Choices–Paths research or lesson.

- **Principles and convenience.** A teaching goal is to help readers act on considered principles, including when doing so is inconvenient. Research how stated values, intuition, incentives and reasoning relate to actual behavior. The view that most people act conveniently rather than rationally or on principle is a question to examine, not an established finding to teach. Also examine how to handle conflicting principles or revise one in light of evidence.
- **The rider and the elephant; the moral matrix.** Jonathan Haidt's *The Righteous Mind* is a preferred starting point. Assess what these models explain, the evidence and criticisms, and whether they can be taught clearly to children. Do not treat the metaphors as literal descriptions of the brain.
- **Creativity before selection.** Explore ways to generate possibilities before judging them. Design thinking is a preferred lead; compare it with other approaches rather than selecting it in advance.
- **When to introduce constraints.** Investigate the concern that filtering options too early can remove ideas that might work. Distinguish assumed limits that can be questioned from real limits that must eventually be addressed. Examine when constraints help or hinder creativity; do not assume that all constraint-based approaches are harmful.

## Lesson folders

Each lesson has a folder named `<subject>-<lesson>`. Its `README.md` lists what belongs there. The `ideas/` subfolder holds thinking that has not been agreed, while `research/` holds the questions and prompts for that lesson. Completed findings go into the shared `research/` library so that other lessons can use them. Numbered product documents stay under `product/`.
