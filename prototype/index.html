A closer look

A head start isn’t the same as a shortcut.

Imagine you have practiced tennis and decide to try another racket sport. Some things may feel familiar. Other things work differently and need new practice.

Earlier learning can give you something to start from. You still have to notice what fits—and what needs changing.

Research on transfer between sports is limited. It does not show that tennis makes every racket sport easy to learn.

The paths we make

Your future has more than one path.

You haven’t lived the rest of your life yet. There is room to learn, change direction, and try again. Your choices matter. So do help, circumstances, and chance.

Imagine a winding line from a beginning to today. Behind today are the route taken and other routes not taken. Ahead are many possible paths. The past cannot be relived, but learning, help and new choices can open different routes from here. Up and down are not scores.

Learning builds on learning.

Earlier understanding can help with a new idea. In a fictional example, Ari learns to share a circle equally, then sees that two quarters equal one half. Ari can use that understanding to measure half a cup using two quarter-cups.

Progress can feel satisfying and encourage another challenge. When it does not, asking for help or trying a different explanation can be part of learning, too. These are possibilities, not guarantees.
