How the paths grow

  1. Two ways for a line to grow.

    Paced gives each line its own next-split time, with quicker early generations. Organic asks each line whether to split as it advances in small age steps. Neither uses a shared wave pattern or a scheduled pause for the whole field.

  2. The Organic rule.

    Advance → look at nearby lines → calculate a split chance → make a seeded random draw → continue or create children. The branching rate combines the base rate, an age taper, a boost near the beginning or Today, and local crowding. The boost decays smoothly over its duration. Each child repeats the same rule independently.

  3. Open widely, then leave room between neighbors.

    Opening spread makes the first one, two or three branching rounds broad. After that, each fan scales with its number of branches: at 12° between neighbors, two branches span 12° and six span 60°. The opening spread remains the upper limit. The count resets at Today. Split chance and spacing still control frequency, independently of shape. A hard branch budget protects responsiveness; crowding nudges nearby lines apart. Crossings are allowed, but a small fork no longer forces a huge change of direction.

  4. Let paths leave the frame.

    Paths can spread upward and downward instead of turning along an invisible wall. They fade at the edge and stop generating just outside the frame. The branch budget stays the same; off-screen paths are not replaced by extra ones.

  5. Grow only through the remaining years.

    The future has a shorter horizon at later ages. Age taper can additionally reduce branching and spread; it is an adjustable illustration, not a measured law of life. Moving Today preserves the original history and regrows possible futures from that point. Gray alternatives fade completely before the Today divider.

These are controls for an illustration—not probabilities of what happens in a person's life.
